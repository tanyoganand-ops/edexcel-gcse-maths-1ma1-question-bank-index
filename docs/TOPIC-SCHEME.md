## Tagging rules used
1. One primary topic per question: the skill the marks mainly reward, decided from the method in the method_note, not the context or wording. Up to three secondary topics for other skills needed.
2. Multi-topic questions: the primary is the final or highest-mark skill step; earlier steps (for example a percentage or ratio step before a trig step) go to secondary.
3. Number versus algebra: a question that works with numbers only is Number; once a letter is manipulated or a formula is rearranged it is Algebra. Index and surd rules are Number when the base is numeric, Algebra when letters are involved.
4. Proof and show-that: algebraic proofs are A15, geometric proofs with reasons are G09 or G10, a numeric show-that goes to the topic of its method. Proof or show-that is noted in method_note, not stored as a separate topic.
5. Problem solving: a multi-step problem set in words is tagged by the method that carries the most marks in the mark scheme, found by reading the scheme privately (not by counting mark codes).
6. Context and diagrams: real-life context does not change the topic. A diagram is only a signal when the diagram is the method (construction, graph drawing, plan and elevation, box plot).
7. Part rows inherit the question topic until reviewed at part level; they are labelled provisional.
8. Overlaps are resolved by the nearest-spec-ref rule: if a topic could be tagged two ways, the one whose spec reference states the exact skill wins, and the other becomes secondary.

## Comparison with Pearson and PMT groupings
- Pearson: the specification content strands (Number, Algebra, Ratio, Geometry, Probability, Statistics) are kept as the top level. Pearson publishes no sub-topic list for past-paper sorting that I found, so sub-topics here are my own, mapped to the spec refs above. Sources: spec PDF p17-32.
- PMT (Physics and Maths Tutor, third-party, https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/maths-revision/gcse-questions-edexcel/ , topic pages fetched 24 Nov 2026 as FETCHED lists of titles): PMT uses six strands with titles such as Four Operations, Primes Factors Multiples, Bounds, Compound Interest, Algebraic Proofs, Solving Using Indices, Sequences of Numbers, Density, Speed, Vectors, Circle Theorems, Congruency and Similarity, Probability Tree Diagrams, Box Plots, Histograms, Sampling. Differences: PMT puts compound interest with fractions, decimals and percentages (same here, N09); in the part of the PMT lists I read (first ~45 titles per strand, not the full lists) I saw no separate title for iteration, functions or equations of circles, where this scheme has A13, A16 and A17 (not confirmed); PMT lists Two Way Tables under Probability, where this scheme has P03 for Venn diagrams (two-way tables not given their own topic here). PMT titles are used only for comparison; no PMT question content is copied.
